A smart home is an ordinary home where some devices can be **monitored, controlled remotely, or run automatically** because they are connected to a network. A bulb you can dim from your phone is a smart device. A bulb that switches itself on at sunset and off when everyone has left is a smart *home* behaviour, because several things are cooperating.

There is no single "smart home product". It is a handful of devices from one or more brands, joined by a control layer you choose.

## The four layers

Every setup, from a single plug to a whole house, has the same four layers. Knowing them makes buying and troubleshooting much easier.

| Layer | What it does | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Devices** | Sense the world or act on it | Bulbs, plugs, door sensors, cameras, locks |
| **Connectivity** | Carries messages between things | Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Zigbee, Z-Wave, Thread |
| **Control** | Where you issue commands | Brand app, voice assistant, wall panel, hub |
| **Logic** | Decides *when* things happen | Schedules, "if this then that" rules, scenes |

When something fails, the problem almost always sits in one layer. "The bulb is offline" is a connectivity problem; "the lights didn't turn on at sunset" is usually a logic problem. See [the offline troubleshooting flow](/learn/device-offline-troubleshooting-flow).

## What a smart home is not

- **Not automatically AI.** Most automations are simple rules: a time, a sensor reading, a person arriving. Rules you can read and change are easier to trust than a black box.
- **Not all-or-nothing.** You can make one lamp smart and stop there. Nothing forces you to convert the whole house.
- **Not always local.** Many devices work through the manufacturer's cloud servers. If the internet or the vendor's service is down, cloud-dependent devices can stop responding. Devices that run locally (over a hub or Matter) keep working. The trade-off is covered in [how smart devices connect](/learn/how-smart-devices-connect).
- **Not a security upgrade by default.** More connected devices means more things to keep updated and secured. See the [security checklist](/learn/secure-your-smart-home-checklist).

## What you need to begin

1. A reliable home Wi-Fi network (see [your home network](/learn/home-network-for-smart-devices)).
2. A smartphone for the device app.
3. One clear problem to solve, such as "I forget to switch off the porch light" or "I want to see who is at the door".

A voice assistant or a hub is optional at the start. Add them when a real need appears, not because a box suggests it.

## Good first projects

- A smart plug on a lamp or fan with a schedule. Check the [plug guide](/learn/smart-plugs) for load limits first.
- A door/window contact sensor that sends a phone alert.
- A single smart bulb in a room where you often walk in carrying things.

Pick one, live with it for a few weeks, and see whether it earns its place before expanding. [Planning your first smart home](/learn/planning-your-first-smart-home) shows how to set goals and a budget, and the [general questions](/learn/general-questions) answer the usual worries about cost, renting and power cuts.

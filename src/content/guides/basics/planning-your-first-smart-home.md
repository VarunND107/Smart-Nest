Most abandoned smart-home gear was bought without a goal. Spend an hour planning and you will waste far less.

## 1. Write down the goals

Pick one or two. The four that cover most households:

- **Security:** know when doors open, see who is outside, lock up remotely.
- **Convenience:** control lights and fans without getting up; voice control.
- **Energy:** stop appliances running when nobody needs them; see what they consume.
- **Automation:** things that happen on their own at the right time.

Each goal points to different devices. Security leans toward [cameras](/learn/smart-cameras), [door sensors](/learn/smart-sensors) and [locks](/learn/smart-locks). Energy leans toward [plugs with monitoring](/learn/energy-monitoring).

## 2. Set a budget in rupees

Decide the *total* you are willing to spend in the first three months, then split it roughly like this:

| Share | Use |
| --- | --- |
| 60-70% | The devices for your main goal |
| 15-20% | Network upgrade if needed (a better router or a mesh node) |
| 10-20% | Keep in reserve for things you discover you need |

The reserve is not optional. People nearly always need a second plug, a mounting kit, or a better Wi-Fi position.

## 3. Start with one room

Choose the room you use most, or the one with the problem you most want to solve. Install, live with it, and note what annoys you. Expanding room by room beats buying for the whole house at once.

## 4. Check the foundations first

Before buying, review [the pre-install checklist](/learn/before-you-install-checklist):

- Does Wi-Fi reach the spots where devices will live?
- Is there a power socket or wiring option where the device must go?
- Do you rent? Retrofit options such as plugs and bulbs are easy to take with you; wiring changes are not.

## 5. Choose an ecosystem

Pick your main app or assistant (see [hubs and ecosystems](/learn/hubs-bridges-and-ecosystems)) and filter your shopping to compatible devices.

## 6. Beware the common traps

- **Buying a hub first.** Wait until you own devices that need one.
- **Ignoring load ratings.** A plug is not a general-purpose switch for any appliance. Read the [plug guide](/learn/smart-plugs).
- **Choosing the cheapest camera with no update policy.** See [firmware and end of support](/learn/firmware-updates-and-end-of-support).
- **Automating everything on day one.** Add one automation at a time so you can tell what caused a problem.

## 7. Review after three months

List what you still use daily. Drop or relocate what you don't. Use the answer to decide the next purchase.

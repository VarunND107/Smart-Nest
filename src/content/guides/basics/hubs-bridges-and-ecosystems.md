Two words cause most of the confusion when people start: **hub** and **ecosystem**.

## What a hub or bridge does

A hub is a small device (or a feature inside a speaker or router) that:

- speaks a protocol your phone cannot, such as Zigbee or Z-Wave;
- keeps devices working and running automations **locally**, without the internet;
- lets many battery devices share one connection.

A *bridge* is a brand-specific hub whose job is to translate that brand's devices for the rest of your home. Wi-Fi devices do not need a hub; Zigbee and Z-Wave devices do. See the [hubs and gateways guide](/learn/hubs-and-gateways) for features to compare.

## What an ecosystem is

An ecosystem is the **control layer** you live in: the app and voice assistant that group your devices into rooms and run routines. The common ones are Google Home, Amazon Alexa, Apple Home and Samsung SmartThings, plus brand apps such as Tapo or Mi Home.

You do not have to pick only one, but running several apps for one home gets messy. Pick a **primary** ecosystem, then buy devices that support it.

## How to choose

1. **Start from your phone and speaker.** If your household is mostly Android, Google Home is the least friction; iPhone households often prefer Apple Home; Alexa works on both and has a wide range of supported devices.
2. **Check support before buying**, using the packaging badge or the vendor's compatibility page. "Works with" claims are specific to a named assistant.
3. **Prefer devices that support Matter** when the price is similar. Matter lets one device be added to more than one ecosystem, so you can change your mind later. See [Matter explained](/learn/matter-explained).
4. **Check if local control is available.** A device that only works through its vendor's cloud is the first to fail in an outage.

## Avoiding lock-in

- Keep a written list of your devices and the account each belongs to. SmartNest's "My home & devices" feature is being built for exactly this.
- Prefer open standards (Matter, Zigbee) over a vendor's private protocol for important devices such as locks and sensors.
- Do not tie critical things (for example door access) to a single company's app being online.

## A sensible default

For most Indian homes starting out: Wi-Fi plugs and bulbs from a mainstream brand, controlled through the voice assistant you already have, with a hub added only when you start using many battery sensors. Revisit the choice after three months of real use.

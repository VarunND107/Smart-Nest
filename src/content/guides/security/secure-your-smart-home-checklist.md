You do not need to be a security expert. Most real-world compromises come from the same few mistakes: reused passwords, outdated firmware and exposed default settings. Work down this list.

## Accounts

1. **Use a unique, long password for every vendor account.** A password manager makes this practical. See [accounts, passwords and 2FA](/learn/accounts-passwords-and-2fa).
2. **Turn on two-factor authentication** wherever it exists, especially for cameras, locks and your voice-assistant account.
3. **Use an email address you control** for device accounts and secure it as well. Account recovery goes through email.

## Router and network

4. **Change the router's admin password** from the default, and change the default Wi-Fi password if it is printed on the router.
5. **Use WPA2 or WPA3** and a long passphrase. Do not use WEP or open Wi-Fi.
6. **Turn off WPS and remote administration** on the router unless you need them.
7. **Update router firmware**, or ask your internet provider to, because it is the front door to everything.
8. **Put smart devices on a guest or separate network** where practical. See [router and network segmentation](/learn/router-and-network-segmentation).

## Devices

9. **Change default device passwords** immediately, particularly on cameras.
10. **Update firmware** and enable automatic updates. Retire devices that no longer receive updates. See [firmware and end of support](/learn/firmware-updates-and-end-of-support).
11. **Disable features you don't use**, such as remote access, public sharing links, cloud storage and microphones.
12. **Review app permissions** on your phone. A plug app rarely needs your contacts or microphone.

## Physical and household

- Keep cameras, locks and hubs out of easy reach of visitors.
- Tell family how the system works, what to do if the Wi-Fi is down, and who has access.
- Before selling or gifting any device, [remove it from your account and reset it](/learn/selling-or-gifting-devices).

## What to buy

- Prefer vendors that publish **update policies** and a way to report vulnerabilities.
- Avoid unbranded devices that need an unknown app or ask for excessive permissions.
- Prefer devices with local control where possible, because they depend less on a vendor's cloud.

## A realistic mindset

No setup is perfectly safe. The aim is to be much harder than the next home, to limit what a single compromised device can reach, and to notice when something is wrong. Read [what devices collect](/learn/data-privacy-what-devices-collect) to understand what data leaves your home, and [camera and microphone privacy](/learn/camera-and-microphone-privacy) for the highest-risk devices.

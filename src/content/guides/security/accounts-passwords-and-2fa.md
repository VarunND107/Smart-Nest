Attackers rarely "hack" a camera. They sign in to the account with a password stolen from another site. Your **accounts** are the most important thing to secure.

## Passwords

- **Unique for each service.** If one service is breached, the rest stay safe.
- **Long beats clever.** A passphrase of four or more random words is easy to type and hard to guess.
- **Use a password manager** to generate and store them. Protect it with a strong master passphrase.
- Do not share passwords in chat or email. Use the product's sharing feature.

## Two-factor authentication (2FA)

2FA asks for a second proof in addition to your password. Best to worst:

| Method | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Passkey or hardware key | Strongest and phishing-resistant where supported |
| Authenticator app (time-based codes) | Strong, works offline |
| SMS code | Better than nothing, but can be intercepted via SIM-swap |

Turn it on for the email account behind your devices, the voice-assistant account, and every camera or lock account. Save the **backup codes** somewhere safe and offline.

## Sharing access with family

- Use the vendor's **household or family sharing**, not a shared password. Each person gets their own login and can be removed alone.
- Give the least access needed: view-only for children or guests where available.
- Review members and shared users every few months.

## Phishing

Messages claiming your camera is offline or your lock is blocked, with a link, are a common trick. Open the **official app** or type the website address yourself rather than following a link. A vendor will not ask for your password by message.

## Check for exposure

- Search your email addresses on a breach-checking service to see if passwords have leaked, and change any that have.
- Review the **active sessions or devices** list in your accounts and sign out ones you do not recognise.

## If you think an account is compromised

1. Change the password from a trusted device. Use a new, unique one.
2. Turn on 2FA, and sign out all other sessions.
3. Check the device's activity log for unfamiliar access, and review shared users and automations for ones you did not create.
4. Remove unknown devices and tokens from the account.
5. Change the password on the email account too, and on any other site where you reused the same password.
6. Contact the vendor's support if you cannot regain control.

A device you pass on may still be tied to your account, hold your Wi-Fi password and keep recordings. Clean it properly, and do it in the right order.

## The order matters

1. **Remove it from the vendor app and account first**, while it is still online. This releases your ownership so the next person can add it. If you factory reset first, some devices stay "claimed" by your account and cannot be set up again.
2. **Remove it from other ecosystems.** Delete it from Google Home, Alexa, Apple Home, SmartThings, and from any Matter controllers it was shared with.
3. **Delete stored data you control.** Cloud recordings, saved maps, voice history and shared links.
4. **Factory reset the device** using the maker's method (hold a button, a pinhole reset or a reset sequence). This clears Wi-Fi credentials and local settings.
5. **Remove removable storage**, such as a microSD card from a camera, and wipe or keep it.
6. **Remove batteries** from locks and sensors if you are not handing them over working.
7. **Check it is truly gone.** Confirm the device no longer appears in your account and your router's list of connected devices.

## Device-specific notes

- **Cameras and doorbells:** download any footage you want to keep first. Then follow the order above. Remove the SD card.
- **Smart locks:** delete all user codes, fingerprints and cards, remove the lock from the account, reset, and keep the **keys**. Better still, do not sell a lock you used on your front door without re-keying the cylinder.
- **Speakers and displays:** sign out, deregister from the account, delete voice history, then factory reset.
- **Robot vacuums:** delete saved maps, then remove the device from the account and reset.
- **Hubs:** removing a hub removes the pairing of every child device, so move or reset those first if you are keeping them.

## Network housekeeping

- Remove old device entries and reserved IP addresses in your router.
- If the device knew your Wi-Fi password and you are unsure it was wiped, **change the Wi-Fi password** and re-join your devices. It is inconvenient but thorough.

## Before discarding a dead device

If it will not power on, you may not be able to reset it. Remove it from your account anyway, change passwords that you used with it, and then dispose of it through a recognised e-waste collection rather than the household bin.

## Buying second-hand

If you buy a used device, **factory reset it and set it up under your own account**. If it refuses to be added, the previous owner has not released it; ask them to remove it from their account.

Energy savings come from stopping **unneeded** use, not from clever gadgets. Start by finding where your electricity goes ([energy monitoring](/learn/energy-monitoring)), then automate the biggest avoidable loads.

## 1. Air conditioner

The AC is usually the largest load.

- **Night schedule:** switch the AC off at a set time, or set a comfortable temperature at bedtime instead of the lowest value.
- **Window open:** if a window contact sensor stays open for 5 minutes, turn the AC off and notify.
- **Pre-cool:** turn it on 15 minutes before you arrive, not all day.
- **How:** use an [IR controller or the AC's own smart feature](/learn/ir-blasters-and-smart-ac), not a standard smart plug.

> **Note:** Each degree you raise the set point usually saves a few percent of cooling energy. Test what is comfortable for you rather than copying numbers from the internet.

## 2. Lights and fans

- Turn off lights and fans when a room has had **no motion for a set period**, using presence or motion sensors.
- Use the **"leaving home"** routine for everything.
- See [lighting automations](/learn/lighting-automations).

## 3. Standby loads

Many devices draw a few watts while "off": TVs, set-top boxes, chargers, audio systems. A [smart plug](/learn/smart-plugs) on a **power strip** with your entertainment gear can switch it all off at night.

- **Check first:** some set-top boxes and routers should not be cut off. A recorder may need to stay on to record, and a network device you need must stay connected.
- Prefer sockets and appliances that **recover cleanly** after power returns.

## 4. Water heater (geyser)

A timer can heat water before you need it instead of keeping it hot all day.

> **Safety:** Do not control a geyser with a plug that is not rated for its load, and do not leave heating appliances to run unattended on any automation. Use a **geyser-rated** controller installed by an electrician, with the appliance's own thermostat and safety cut-off intact.

## 5. Charging

Switch off chargers after a set number of hours so devices are not left on charge overnight needlessly, within the limits of what is safe for that device. Check the device's own guidance.

## Measure the effect

1. Record a week of baseline usage from a plug, a meter or your bill.
2. Apply **one change**.
3. Compare in similar weather and occupancy.
4. Keep what works. Remove what does not.

## Be realistic

An automation that saves a few rupees a month is fine if it costs nothing, but not if it adds risk or complexity. Focus on the large loads. See [designing automations safely](/learn/designing-automations-safely) before you automate anything that gets hot.

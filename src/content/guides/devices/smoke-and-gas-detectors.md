Smoke, LPG (cooking gas) and carbon-monoxide detectors protect lives. Connected versions add a phone notification when you are away from home. That is useful, but it does not change what the device is for.

> **Safety:** A smart detector's loud local alarm is what protects people in the house. The phone alert is a bonus. Never depend on an app notification as your only warning, and never disable the local siren to "reduce noise".

## What connected detectors add

- A notification to your phone and other family members when an alarm sounds.
- Alerts for low battery or a failing sensor.
- Optional automations, such as turning on lights when smoke is detected.

## Pick the right detector for the hazard

| Hazard | Detector | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fire | Smoke detector (photoelectric or combined) | Place on ceilings, outside sleeping areas |
| Cooking-gas leak | LPG/PNG gas detector | Mount **near the ceiling for natural gas (lighter than air)** and **low near the floor for LPG (heavier than air)**; follow the maker's instructions |
| Carbon monoxide | CO detector | Needed with gas geysers and in poorly ventilated spaces |

Gas behaviour differs, so the placement instruction in the product manual matters more than any general rule.

## Buy certified, check the sensor life

Look for recognised safety certification suitable for your region. Detectors have a **limited sensor life** (often several years). The product should show an expiry or replacement date. Replace it on time even if it seems to work.

## Mains or battery

Mains-powered detectors with battery backup keep working in a power cut. A detector that only runs from the Wi-Fi socket will go quiet during an outage. If the unit needs wiring, hire a licensed electrician; see [installing a smart switch safely](/learn/installing-a-smart-switch-safely) for the same wiring cautions.

## Test regularly

1. Press the test button monthly and confirm the local alarm *and* the phone alert.
2. Check battery warnings and replace promptly.
3. Do not test with real gas or open flames near the sensor.
4. Keep detectors clean; dust and cooking vapour cause false alarms.

## About automations

Some people connect a gas detector to a motorised shut-off valve. Only attempt that with a product designed and certified for it, installed by a professional. Do not improvise a shut-off with a smart plug. See [designing automations safely](/learn/designing-automations-safely).

A smart switch replaces (or sits behind) a standard wall switch and controls the **circuit**, not a single bulb. That means every bulb, fan or fixture on the circuit becomes controllable, and the wall switch still works for guests and family.

## Two forms

- **Replacement smart switch/panel:** replaces your existing switchboard plate. Common as touch panels with several channels.
- **Relay or module:** a small unit that goes *behind* the existing switch inside the switch box. The old switch stays and tells the relay what to do.

Relays are popular for retrofit because the wall plate stays as it is, which suits rentals and avoids a mismatch with your switchboard design.

## The neutral wire question

Many Wi-Fi smart switches need a **neutral wire** at the switch box to power their electronics. In many Indian homes the switch box carries only the live feed to the light, so neutral may not be present there. Devices designed for a two-wire setup exist, but they have their own limits, for example minimum load requirements that can make LED lamps flicker or glow.

> **Safety:** Do not guess about wiring. Mains wiring can injure or kill and a bad connection can start a fire. Have a licensed electrician check for neutral at each box before you buy, and read [installing a smart switch safely](/learn/installing-a-smart-switch-safely).

## Fan control

Fans need a **speed-control-capable** module designed for fans. A plain on/off relay can run a fan at one speed only, and using the wrong module with a regulator can damage either. Choose a module explicitly labelled for fan speed control.

## Load and quality

- Check the rated load per channel and keep within it. Inductive loads like fans and motors need more headroom than LEDs.
- Prefer a recognised safety certification and clear specifications.
- Look for what the switch does on a power cut and when the internet is down. A good one keeps the physical switch working.

## Why choose switches over bulbs

| | Smart switch | Smart bulb |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wall switch still works | Yes | Not if turned off |
| Works with any bulb | Yes | No, bulb is the device |
| Colour changing | No | Yes |
| Install effort | Wiring | None |

If you want colour or no wiring, choose [bulbs](/learn/smart-bulbs). If you want reliable control of whole circuits, choose switches. See [lighting automations](/learn/lighting-automations) for what to do next.

A smart speaker adds voice control to your home and often doubles as a **controller** or a hub for other devices. A smart display adds a screen for cameras, timers, recipes and video calls.

## What it does for a smart home

- Lets you control devices by voice and by room.
- Runs your routines ("good night" turns off lights and sets the alarm).
- In some models, acts as a Matter controller, a Thread border router or a Zigbee hub. Check the specification; do not assume.
- Announces sensor events or doorbell presses.

## How the microphone works

A smart speaker listens continuously for a **wake word** processed on the device. Audio is meant to be sent to the cloud only *after* it hears the wake word. Wake-word misfires do happen, so assistant recordings can include things you did not mean to say.

Practical controls:

- Use the **hardware mute switch** when you want certainty.
- Review and delete voice history in the assistant's privacy settings.
- Turn off features you do not use, such as purchases by voice, or require a PIN.

More in [camera and microphone privacy](/learn/camera-and-microphone-privacy).

## Speaker or display?

| Choose a speaker when | Choose a display when |
| --- | --- |
| You want voice control cheaply | You want to see doorbell or camera video |
| It will live in a bedroom (no camera) | You use it for recipes and timers in the kitchen |
| You want several rooms covered | You want visual confirmation of actions |

> **Note:** Some displays have cameras. In a bedroom, choose a model with a physical camera shutter or no camera.

## Placement

- Keep it away from corners and windows and roughly head-height or above, so it hears you and hears you *less* from outside.
- One per main room works better than one for the whole house.
- Do not put a speaker where a visitor can issue commands, such as next to the front door, if it controls locks or alarms.

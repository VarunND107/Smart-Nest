A video doorbell combines a button, camera, speaker and microphone so you can see and talk to whoever is at the door from your phone.

## Wired or battery

- **Wired:** powered from a low-voltage doorbell transformer or a plug-in adapter. Always powered, so features like pre-roll recording are more reliable. Needs compatible wiring.
- **Battery:** quick to fit with screws or adhesive. Needs periodic charging, and may wake only on motion, which can mean a short delay before you see video.

Many Indian homes use a mains-voltage (230 V) doorbell button with a separate chime, not a low-voltage system. A video doorbell designed for low voltage must **not** be connected to mains. Check the specification and ask an electrician if unsure.

## Chime compatibility

A video doorbell's own chime is in the phone app and sometimes in the unit. To get a sound **inside** the house you may need a plug-in chime that comes with the product, a smart speaker announcement, or a compatible mechanical chime. Check this before buying.

## Mounting and field of view

Aim to cover the doorstep and a bit of the approach. Mount at about chest height of the person you expect, usually 1.2 to 1.5 m. Direct sunlight and strong backlight make faces dark; a shaded position or a wedge mount helps.

## Where does the video go?

Same options as other [smart cameras](/learn/smart-cameras): onboard storage, cloud subscription, or a local recorder. A doorbell that needs a subscription to save clips will not save anything without it, so confirm the pricing model.

## Privacy specific to doorbells

- A camera at a **shared door** (apartment corridor, common landing) can record neighbours constantly. Narrow the view and use motion zones if the product supports them.
- Check whether the camera records audio. Turn it off if not needed.
- Do not post footage of identifiable people publicly. See [camera and microphone privacy](/learn/camera-and-microphone-privacy).

## Pairing with a lock

You can automate "when the doorbell is pressed, turn the porch light on". Do **not** set a doorbell to unlock a door. See [smart locks](/learn/smart-locks) and [designing automations safely](/learn/designing-automations-safely).

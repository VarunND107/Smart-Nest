You cannot cut what you cannot see. Energy monitoring turns "the bill is high" into "this appliance uses this much".

## Ways to monitor

- **Smart plugs with energy monitoring** measure the power (watts) and cumulative energy (kWh) of whatever is plugged in. Best for individual appliances up to the plug's rating.
- **Clamp or DIN-rail energy meters** measure a whole circuit or the whole house at the distribution board. Installation involves the live wiring, so it should be done by a licensed electrician.
- **Smart meter data** from your electricity provider, where available, gives overall usage by hour or day in their app.

## Units that matter

- **Watt (W):** how much power an appliance draws right now.
- **Kilowatt-hour (kWh):** energy used over time. Your bill is based on this. An appliance drawing 1,000 W for one hour uses 1 kWh.
- Rough cost = kWh × your tariff per unit. Tariffs vary by state and by slab, so use the rate on your own bill.

## What to measure first

1. **Always-on loads:** router, set-top box, chargers, TVs in standby. Individually small but they run 24 hours.
2. **Heavy but intermittent loads:** water heater, microwave, washing machine, iron.
3. **Air conditioners:** the biggest item in many homes. A plug is not suitable (see [IR and AC control](/learn/ir-blasters-and-smart-ac)); use the AC's own energy display, a circuit meter, or your bill pattern.

## Reading the data

- Note the **standby draw** when an appliance is "off". Many devices draw a few watts continuously.
- Compare a week of readings before and after a change, in similar weather, to see whether it helped.
- Be cautious about accuracy. Cheap plugs are accurate enough for comparisons but may drift on very small loads.

## Turning data into savings

- Switch off appliances that waste standby power on a schedule using [smart plugs](/learn/smart-plugs), only where safe.
- Shift flexible loads (washing, charging) to times that suit your tariff if your provider offers time-of-day pricing.
- Use [energy-saving automations](/learn/energy-saving-automations) to stop AC and lights running in empty rooms.

Be honest with yourself: a smart plug that saves ₹20 a month on one appliance does not repay a large purchase. Spend where the data shows a big, steady load.

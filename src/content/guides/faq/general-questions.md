## Do I need to spend a lot to start?

No. A single smart plug or bulb is a complete first project. Most people are better served by a handful of well-chosen devices than a whole-house setup. See [planning your first smart home](/learn/planning-your-first-smart-home).

## Do I need the internet?

For remote control, notifications and cloud features, yes. For devices that support **local control** (many hub-based and Matter devices), basic control and automations can keep working when the internet is down. Check each product's description.

## Will it work during a power cut?

Not unless the devices and your router have power. A small UPS or DC backup for the router and hub keeps the network up through short cuts. Lights and fans controlled by a smart switch should still work with the **physical switch**.

## Can I do it if I rent?

Yes, with devices that need no wiring: plugs, bulbs, battery sensors, IR controllers, plug-in cameras and removable lock retrofits. Avoid changing fixed wiring without the landlord's written approval, and take the devices with you when you leave.

## Do I need an electrician?

Only for anything that touches fixed wiring: in-wall switches, fan modules, hard-wired doorbells, energy meters at the distribution board. Plug-in and screw-in devices do not need one. See [installing a smart switch safely](/learn/installing-a-smart-switch-safely).

## Is it hard to set up?

Pairing one Wi-Fi device usually takes ten minutes. The harder part is the **network**: weak signal and 2.4 GHz problems cause most issues. See [connecting a device to Wi-Fi](/learn/connecting-a-device-to-wifi) and [home network for smart devices](/learn/home-network-for-smart-devices).

## Does a smart home use a lot of electricity?

Each device draws very little, typically a watt or two on standby. The savings come from controlling larger loads such as air conditioners. Measure first ([energy monitoring](/learn/energy-monitoring)).

## Will my smart home be hacked?

Risk is real but manageable. Unique passwords, 2FA, updates and a hardened router remove most of it. See the [security checklist](/learn/secure-your-smart-home-checklist).

## Is this AI?

Not unless it says so. Most smart-home behaviour is simple rules such as "at sunset, turn on the light". SmartNest's planner is **rule-based logic**, not AI.

## What does SmartNest do, exactly?

SmartNest is a student project that offers guides, a place to record your home and devices, a rule-based planner, and a demonstration support-ticket system. It **does not control your devices** or read their live status, and its device details and prices are sample data.

## What should I buy first?

A smart plug for a lamp, a door sensor or a single smart bulb. Pick the problem you care about most. Then live with it for a few weeks.

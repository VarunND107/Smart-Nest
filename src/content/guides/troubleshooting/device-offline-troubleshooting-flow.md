"Offline" means the app cannot reach the device. Work through these steps in order and stop when it comes back. Change one thing at a time so you know what fixed it.

## Step 1: Is it just this device?

Check whether **other devices** are offline too.

- **All devices offline:** the problem is your router, internet or the vendor's cloud. Go to step 2.
- **One device offline:** the problem is local to it. Go to step 3.
- **All devices of one brand offline:** likely the vendor's service or their hub. Check the maker's status page or social account and wait.

## Step 2: Check the internet and router

1. Open a website on your phone using mobile data off and Wi-Fi on. If it fails, your internet is down.
2. Check the router's lights. Restart it: unplug for 30 seconds, plug back in, and wait about five minutes.
3. If you changed the Wi-Fi name or password recently, devices must be re-joined. See [after a power cut or router change](/learn/after-a-power-cut-or-router-change).
4. Check the router is not overloaded and the signal reaches the device. See [Wi-Fi and connectivity problems](/learn/wifi-and-connectivity-problems).

## Step 3: Check the device

1. **Power.** Is it plugged in, switched on at the wall, and is the socket live? For battery devices, replace the battery with a fresh one.
2. **Indicator light.** Check the manual for what it means. A blinking light often means "can't find Wi-Fi" or "pairing mode".
3. **Distance and obstacles.** If the device moved, or something large was placed near it, signal may have dropped.
4. **Power-cycle.** Unplug for 10 seconds and plug back in. Wait two minutes.

## Step 4: Check the app and account

1. Force close the app, reopen it, and pull to refresh.
2. Check you are signed in to the right account.
3. Update the app and the device's firmware ([firmware guide](/learn/firmware-updates-and-end-of-support)).
4. Check whether the device appears in the router's list of connected devices. If it does, the device is connected and the problem is the cloud or app; if not, it never joined Wi-Fi.

## Step 5: Re-join or re-pair

1. If it **never rejoins Wi-Fi**, put it back in pairing mode and set it up again ([connecting a device to Wi-Fi](/learn/connecting-a-device-to-wifi)). Remember the 2.4 GHz rule.
2. If it **connects but the app shows offline**, remove it from the app and add it again.

## Step 6: Last resorts

1. Factory reset the device and set it up fresh. Record any settings first.
2. Test the device on a **different Wi-Fi network** (a phone hotspot works) to see whether the device or your network is the cause.
3. Contact the maker's support with the model, firmware version and what you tried. See [when to reset or ask for help](/learn/when-to-reset-or-ask-for-help).

## Prevent repeats

- Give important devices a [DHCP reservation](/learn/home-network-for-smart-devices) so their address does not change.
- Keep the router, hub and devices updated.
- Put the router (and hub) on a small UPS if your area has frequent power cuts.

Voice control feels magical when it works and maddening when it doesn't. Almost all the difference is in naming.

## Name devices by room and role

Use short, distinct names that you would naturally say.

| Poor | Better |
| --- | --- |
| Smart Plug 1 | Bedroom lamp |
| Light | Study ceiling light |
| Tapo_P100_3F | Hall fan |
| AC | Bedroom AC |

Rules that help:

- **Two or three words** at most.
- **No near-duplicates** such as "Lamp" and "Lamps".
- **Avoid names that sound like commands** ("Turn", "Set").
- **Avoid unusual spellings** the assistant cannot pronounce.

## Assign rooms

Put every device in a room in the assistant app. Then "turn off the lights in the bedroom" works without naming each device. Make sure the room name matches how you speak. If you use more than one language, test the names you actually say.

## Use groups for sets

Create a group such as "Living room lights" for bulbs that should always act together. Groups also let a routine act on one item instead of ten, which is faster and less error-prone.

## Make routines readable

Name routines by the phrase you will say: "Good night", "Leaving home". Keep each routine small and test it with the app before relying on it by voice.

## What to keep off voice

> **Safety:** Do not allow voice commands to unlock doors, disable alarms or open garage gates without a spoken PIN or a second check. Sound travels through windows, and a TV or a visitor can issue commands.

Also be careful with heating appliances and anything where an accidental "on" is risky.

## Multiple speakers and rooms

- Keep one speaker per room that you use often.
- Check that the speaker responds to the **nearest** device. If two speakers answer at once, adjust their sensitivity or room assignment.
- Place speakers away from the TV.

A camera in the wrong place gives you hours of footage of nothing useful. Planning for ten minutes saves a lot of re-mounting.

## Decide what each camera is for

Write one sentence per camera: "See who comes to the front door", "Watch the gate and parked cars". The purpose decides height and angle.

## Outdoor positions

- **Entrances first:** the main door, back door, and the gate.
- Mount at about **2.5 to 3 m** so it is out of easy reach but still shows faces. Higher mounts see roofs and the tops of heads.
- Angle **down** slightly and aim along the approach path so people walk toward the camera, which shows faces better than a side view.
- Avoid pointing at **bright sky, streetlights or the rising/setting sun**; backlight turns people into silhouettes.
- Use a **weather-rated** camera (check the IP rating) and a drip loop in the cable so water does not run into the connector.

## Indoor positions

- Common areas such as the hall or entrance, not bedrooms or bathrooms.
- Place on a shelf or mount at about head height or above, looking toward doors and windows.
- Avoid pointing through **glass**. IR night vision reflects off windows and washes out the image.

## Power and data

| Option | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Mains adapter | Needs a weatherproof socket near the mount outdoors |
| PoE | One Ethernet cable carries data and power; needs a PoE switch or injector |
| Battery | Easy to place; check recharge frequency and recording limits |

Wi-Fi cameras should be tested at the exact mounting spot **before** drilling. Check signal strength, and consider a mesh node or a wired camera if it is poor. See [home network guide](/learn/home-network-for-smart-devices).

## Test before you drill

1. Hold the camera at the planned spot and view the live feed.
2. Walk the approach path to check coverage and faces.
3. Test at night to see IR range and reflections.
4. Only then mark and drill.

## Respect people's privacy

- Do not capture neighbours' doors, windows or private areas. Use **privacy masks or detection zones** if the camera has them.
- Do not put a camera where it records people in a state of undress, including in guest rooms.
- Tell household members and visitors where cameras are installed. More in [camera and microphone privacy](/learn/camera-and-microphone-privacy).

## Finish

Change the default password, enable two-factor authentication, set a sensible motion zone to reduce false alerts, and check the retention setting for stored video. If it behaves badly afterwards, see [camera troubleshooting](/learn/camera-troubleshooting).
